Student-Made Vocabulary List

A comprehensive list of important vocabulary terms related to college admissions and the application process.

Letters A-H

  • Acceptance Rate: The percentage of applicants who are admitted to a college. May impact a college's selectivity and competitiveness.
  • Accreditation: A process by which a college or university is evaluated and recognized as meeting certain standards of quality. May impact a college's reputation and the value of its degrees.
  • ACT (American College Testing): A standardized test that is commonly used for college admissions in the United States. Deals in more memorization of facts/formulas than the SAT, and is viewed as less academically rigorous. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Admission: The process of being accepted to a college.
  • Advanced Placement (AP): College-level courses offered in high school that can earn students college credit.
  • Application: The process of applying to a college, which typically includes submitting an application form, transcripts, test scores, and other materials.
  • Application Fee: A fee that is required to submit a college application. May impact a student's ability to apply to multiple colleges.
  • Award Letter: A document that informs a student of their financial aid package.
  • Common App: The most commonly used online application platform for college admissions. May be refrenced for college application data or information.
  • Early Action: A way to apply to a college early without commiting to attend that college.May impact that college's admissions decision.
  • Early Decision: A way to apply to a college early with the committment to attend that college if accepted.May impact that college's admissions decision.
  • Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA): The standard way to apply for federal financial aid for college. Compiles all financial information for a family and is what all colleges use to determine how much financial aid a student needs.
  • Financial Aid: Money that is given or loaned to students to help pay for college. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • GPA (Grade Point Average): A measure of a student's academic performance, calculated as the average of their grades. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Holistic Review: A method of evaluating college applications that considers multiple factors beyond just grades and test scores.
  • Hybrid Course(s): Courses that combine both online and in-person instruction.

Letters I-P

  • In-State Tuition: The tuition rate that is charged to students who are residents of the state where the college is located. May impact a student's ability to afford a college outside of their state.
  • Legacy Applicant: A student who has a family member (usually a parent or grandparent) who attended the same college. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Loan: Money that is borrowed and must be repaid with interest. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Merit-Based Scholarship: A scholarship that is awarded based on a student's academic or extracurricular achievements, rather than financial need. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Need-Based Financial Aid: Financial aid that is awarded based on a student's financial need, as determined by the FAFSA. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Need-Blind Admissions: A policy in which colleges do not consider a student's financial situation when making admissions decisions. Extremely rare. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Net Price: The total cost of attendance at a college minus any financial aid or scholarships received. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Out-of-State Tuition: The tuition rate that is charged to students who are not residents of the state where the college is located. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Personal Statement: An essay that is typically required as part of a college application, in which students can share more about themselves and their experiences. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Private University: A university that is not funded by the government and typically charges higher tuition than public universities. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.
  • Public University: A university that is funded by the government and typically charges lower tuition than private universities. May impact a student's ability to afford a college.

Letters Q-Z

  • Recommendation Letter: A letter written by a teacher or counselor that speaks to a student's character, abilities, and potential for success in college. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Rolling Admissions: A type of admissions process in which colleges review applications as they are received and make decisions on a rolling basis, rather than waiting until after the application deadline. May impact when a student receives an admissions decision.
  • SAT (Scholastic Assessment Test): A standardized test that is commonly used for college admissions in the United States. Tends to be more heavily weighted in admissions decisions. Deals in more academic curiosity than straight memorization of facts. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Superscore
    • A policy carried by most colleges in which they take the highest section scores from all of a student's SAT/ACT tests and combines them to create the highest score possible. Is always a benefit and is carried by all colleges otherwise stated(on this site).
  • Test-Optional: A policy in which colleges do not require applicants to submit standardized test scores (such as the SAT or ACT) as part of their application. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Transcript: An official record of a student's academic performance, including courses taken and grades received. May impact a college's admissions decision.
  • Undergraduate: A student who is pursuing a bachelor's degree at a college or university. May impact a student's ability to apply to certain colleges or programs.
